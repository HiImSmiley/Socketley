An mqtt runtime is an MQTT broker for 3.1, 3.1.1 and 5.0 on TCP, TLS and WebSocket: devices, apps and browsers publish to topics and subscribe to them, and a client that goes away keeps its session, its subscriptions and the messages sent while it was gone, until it comes back or the session expires. Rules of its own hand messages to a cache, a db, a storage, a server or another broker, and a storage's events and a script's messages reach its subscribers.

socketley mqtt passwd /etc/socketley/mqtt.users sensor-17
socketley create mqtt broker --users /etc/socketley/mqtt.users \
    --acl /etc/socketley/mqtt.acl --ws 8083 --persistent on -s
mosquitto_sub -h 127.0.0.1 -u sensor-17 -P secret -t 'home/#'

What it does

  • The protocol: QoS 0, 1 and 2, retained messages, wills with 5.0's delay, sessions with 3.1.1's clean session and 5.0's expiry, shared subscriptions, topic aliases, message expiry and the 5.0 properties.
  • Ports: 1883, or 8883 with --tls, and WebSocket on --ws; with --ca a verified client certificate's name is the user.
  • Who: a password file (--users, written by socketley mqtt passwd), access tokens of an idp, or --anonymous; topic rights per user in --acl.
  • Keeping: --persistent keeps sessions and retained messages across restarts; --session-queue, --retained-max, --max-sessions and --max-packet bound them.
  • Rules: --routes hands matching messages to other runtimes; socketley.mqtt.publish publishes from any script.
  • Operating it: socketley <broker> pub|sub|sessions|kick|retained.
